## 1. What was wrong

Amendment 1 §1 records the founder's prediction for Q2, that a non-standard stream is allowed, as "given after he had
seen the session's prediction in the question, so not independent of it". The timing is true: the question that asked
for his prediction showed the session's first. The inference was the session's, and it was wrong. **His prediction is
independent, and the ledger scores it as his.**

## 2. Whose question Q2 is

Amendment 1 presents Q2 as one of the session's recommendations. **The idea is Thon Ly's, and it is on the record
nineteen days earlier.** In the session that began the formal-Abhidhamma program (2026-09-27; the archive is private
and carries an OpenTimestamps proof), he wrote that the integral "is the summation of infinitesimal points", and he
asked whether "the mathematical 'bridge' that can smoothly integrate over the choppy quantum points into Einstein's
continuous curve can be found in the Abhidhamma". A discrete structure whose points, infinitely many between any two
far apart, add up to a continuum is what a non-standard stream is. The session read that sentence as a claim about
physics, answered it as one, and missed the mathematics. So **Q2 is credited to Thon Ly, posed 2026-09-27.** The other
three questions, and the form all four are asked in, are the session's.

## 3. What changes in how predictions are asked

Asking for the founder's prediction in a question that already shows the session's makes his independence impossible
to check, whatever the truth. From now on the founder's prediction is asked **before** the session states its own.
